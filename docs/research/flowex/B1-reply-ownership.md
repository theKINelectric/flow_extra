# B1 — reply ownership (FX-005)

Experiment record, pursuit §3 style. Work package B, first half; design per Astra's consult (codex session `01a0e072`, 2026-09-26).

- **Claim:** a call's reply destination is revocable, one resolution serves both monitor and submission, and the deadline travels with the packet — no stage begins work after the caller's budget is gone, an arrived answer beats the deadline, and anything later is dropped rather than delivered into a dead wait.
- **Counterexample (audited revision `1e06223`):** the consumer sent the finished packet to the original caller PID; the audit probe caught a 1ms deadline on an 80ms callback and found `LATE_REPLY present` — repeated caught timeouts accumulated packets in long-lived callers. Nothing carried the caller's deadline into the pipeline either, so post-deadline dequeues ran callbacks on abandoned work.
- **Regression:** `test/flowex/pipeline/reply_ownership_test.exs` (+ `test/support/reply_pipelines.ex`). RED at `2061f68` — 0/3. Two trap-craft lessons are recorded there: a completion barrier alone is one demand hop too early (the negative needs a settle window), and a 1ms deadline races the first dequeue itself (deterministic margins: dequeue well inside the budget, completion far outside — 40ms deadline against 200ms work).
- **Fix (aliases, per the consult):** `%Flowex.IP{}` carries `reply_to` (a process alias owned by the caller), `deadline` (absolute local monotonic ms, `nil` = never), and `expired`. The generated async `call/3` resolves the consumer **once** (`Flowex.Pipeline.resolve_consumer!/2`), monitors and casts to that same PID, and mints the alias; every ending — reply, `:DOWN`, deadline — demonitors and unaliases; the timeout path first checks whether this exact request's answer already beat the clock (it wins) without touching unrelated messages. `Flowex.Stage` and the sync walker skip expired packets before any callback or error handler, marking `expired: true`; `Flowex.Pipeline.unwrap!/2` turns an expired reply into the caller's own `:timeout`. The sync `call/3` passes `remaining/1` to its `GenServer.call` and stamps the deadline on the packet. Casts carry neither destination nor deadline.
- **Presentation note:** caller death mid-wait auto-revokes the alias (process exit), so orphaned replies are dropped by the VM with no cleanup code of ours. Deadline fields are one-node-local by construction and documented as such on `Flowex.IP`.
- **Commands:** `mix test test/flowex/pipeline/reply_ownership_test.exs` → 3/3; `mix test --seed 0/42/99` → 92 passed each; format, compile `--warnings-as-errors`, dialyzer (0), credo --strict (0) clean. Runtime: Elixir 1.20.4 / OTP 29.
